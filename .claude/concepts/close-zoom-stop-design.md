# Close-zoom stop design

## Problem

We have three zoom levels of stop representation. Pill-zoom is finished, far-zoom dots are near done. Close-zoom (high zoom levels) previously had no dedicated design — it just inherited the pill-zoom rendering, which wastes the available screen space at z17+. At close zoom we have room to show platform-level information: which line stops where, in which direction, toward which destination, and what the actual extent of the station is.

## Requirements

### Activation and zoom bands

- Replaces pill-zoom rendering from z17 upward. Hard cut at the z16 → z17 boundary; no fade overlap.
- Five zoom bands with band-specific content. The arrow does **not** grow across bands (all bands are equally long in metres) — zooming in itself provides the extra pixels, which the higher bands spend on destination text while glyph heights (in metres) shrink:
  - **Band A (z17)**: very small pill-arrow, solid in the line color with a white border, containing only the centered line number. No disc, no destination.
  - **Band B (z18)**: slim single-line pill-arrow — white body, border in the line color, colored disc at the round end with the line number, one line of destination text.
  - **Band C (z19)**: same footprint as band B, destination may wrap to two lines; smaller glyph height (in metres) so the zoom gain is absorbed.
  - **Band D (z20)**: same footprint as C, up to three lines of destination, glyph height shrunk again.
  - **Band E (z21+)**: same footprint as C, up to four lines of destination, smallest glyph height.
- Band switches are hard cuts at integer zooms (A→B at z18, B→C at z19, C→D at z20, D→E at z21).

### Pill-arrow shape

The visual element placed beside the line for each line+direction at a stop.

- Rounded rectangle ("pill") where the going-to end is replaced by a chevron tip — only the tip is pointed (a `>` shape); the opposite end stays rounded.
- The body is **straight**, with a per-pill axis: each pill-arrow derives its angle from the **stop position line at its own segment** (see Placement). A single-point tangent at the stop was tried first and tilted whole stacks against the line near bends; backward extrapolation of the raw line geometry at terminals produced guesswork angles. Curved bodies were also tried and retired — labels cannot follow the curve, so text and shape angles diverge on bent lines.
- Chevron tip points in the direction of travel away from this stop.
- The occupied length (back cap + body + tip) is exact — stacking math accounts for the rounded cap's bulge.
- Geometry must render crisply through z22 (dense vertices, no tile simplification of the shapes).
- **Duo-tone design (bands B–E)**: white body, border in the line color, a disc in the line color at the round end containing the line number, destination text in black on the white body.
- **Solid design (band A)**: whole pill in the line color, white border, line number only.
- Line numbers are always bold, white, and sized to fit their container.

### Text

- Destination text is left-aligned within its text region (anchored at the disc side; at the chevron side when the label is flipped for readability). Labels rotate with the pill axis and flip 180° when they would render upside-down.
- The text region grants slightly more clearance on the disc side and extends slightly into the chevron base on the arrow side.
- Glyph heights are defined in metres and convert to pixels on the map's zoom curve, so text scales exactly with the pill geometry and can never overflow it. Uniform size within a band, with one exception: wide line numbers (e.g. "IR15") shrink per feature just enough to fit the disc; short numbers keep the band's nominal size. *(Pending re-implementation after the git restore.)*
- All text fitting (wrapping, word abbreviation, ellipsis placement) measures real per-character advance widths from the baked Noto Sans metrics table (`glyph_widths.json`, regular for destinations / bold for numbers, kerning ignored) — no flat character counting. The two-line ellipsis is placed after computing the actual line break, trimmed until the last line truly fits.
- Line breaks are computed at build time (baked into the label). Words longer than a line are **abbreviated with a single dot** — no hyphen splitting, since without linguistic hyphenation the break positions would be nonsense. Text exceeding the band's line budget (B: one line, C: two lines) ends with an ellipsis.
- **Ellipsis reflow**: when the wrapped text overflows the band's line budget, the ellipsis must letter-fill the last visible line, not stop at a word boundary. Word-wrapping happens first, so a word that overflows is bumped whole to the next line; if that next line is then discarded (e.g. a one-line band), the last kept line could be left far short of the available width — "La Roche FR" on a one-line band would strand as "La…". The requirement: on overflow, the last kept line is rejoined with every dropped line and letter-filled to the line width before the ellipsis, so the cut lands where the characters actually run out ("La Roche FR" → "La Roc…"). This applies to every band; on multi-line bands only the final visible line is affected.
- **Destination shortening**: if the destination begins with the current stop's city (comma- or space-separated — "Bern, …" or "Bern …" on a pill in Bern), the city prefix is stripped; the separator requirement keeps names like "Berneck" intact. If a comma remains afterwards, everything from the comma on is dropped ("Wabern, Tram-Endstation" → "Wabern"). A destination that is exactly the city name is never emptied.
- **Loop-line destinations**: for lines whose first and last stop are the same station (loop lines), the terminus is useless as a destination — at the terminus every pill would name the very stop it sits at (canonical case: Bad Zurzach buses 1–4, all loops from Bahnhof, all showing "Bahnhof" at Bahnhof). Instead each loop gets an **apex stop**: pills at stops **before** the apex show the apex as destination; pills at the apex and all later stops show the terminus. The apex is the stop **geographically furthest from the terminus**. Stops sharing the terminus station (UIC) are never apex candidates — loops may pass through the terminus mid-route (Bad Zurzach bus 4), and picking that call would relabel the whole outbound leg with the terminus name. The chosen apex name goes through the normal destination-shortening rules. Non-loop lines are unaffected. *(An importance-score-within-middle-third rule was tried first and retired: the station importance score is rail-dominated and useless for ranking city-bus loop stops — it sent line 3 to "Höfli" instead of "Rietheim, Bahnhof" and let line 4 pick the terminus itself.)*

### Pill-arrow placement

One pill-arrow per (stop, line, direction). Same-direction variants of a line at a stop collapse into a single pill-arrow listing all their (deduplicated, shortened) destinations. **Departures only**: a line's final stop is an arrival and gets no pill-arrow there.

- **Layover-departure dedup**: a line's first stop gets no pill-arrow when that stop has no platform code and the same line calls again at the same station (UIC) at a platform-coded stop later in the run — the platform-coded call is the real departure and keeps the pill. This is the departure-side mirror of the pill/dot arrival-drop rule; canonical case is Bern bus 30 departing the bare layover point and then serving platform A of the same station. The dot-side departure-skip rule (skip whenever a sibling arrives at the same stop_id) must NOT be copied here — close zoom has no arrival pills to fall back on, so it would erase lines from their termini.
- **Stop position line**: the queue's backbone is the stop's position line — **re-used** from the stop/dot placement, not recomputed: the same fitted-to-the-line slice of real geometry that the debug stop lines draw (for rail, the platform extent). That logic automatically finds the line whose geometry actually covers the stop's ground — at a terminal it draws the stop along the **arrival** line, whose geometry approaches along the street and ends at the stop, so the slice covers exactly the ground behind the stop where the departing queue stands. Nothing is ever extrapolated. Each pill-arrow derives its angle from the stop position line **at its own segment** (the average direction of the part its span covers). Where the stack extends beyond the range, it continues **dead straight**: each pill takes the angle of the last pill-arrow that fit and continues in that direction. At the standard bus/tram platform length about 3.5 pill-arrows fit, enough for ~90% of stops — straight continuation is the exception. Angles are never derived from single-point end tangents or from backward extrapolation of the raw line geometry.
- **Terminal platform stretch**: at a terminal stop of tram or bus, when the combined pill-arrow stack is longer than the stop position line, the stop position line is extended — for close-zoom display only — toward the side where line geometry is available, as far as necessary to fit the stack. This replaces the dead-straight continuation for these terminal cases; non-terminal stops keep the dead-straight rule. *(Depends on `stop-extent-osm-walk.md`, which makes extents end where real geometry ends.)*
- **Aerial + funicular terminals**: aerial cable-car stations and funicular terminals are almost always the only stops on their line (cable cars and funiculars typically run endpoint-to-endpoint), and both are point-like — no physical platform-ground stretches along the vehicle path. At a terminal stop, the pill-arrow sits **against the polyline's endpoint** — same rule structure as end-of-platform rail terminals: the fastest pill's back-cap coincides with the line's endpoint and any further pills queue inward from there. The line visually starts with the pill-arrow. At non-terminal stops (V-Bahn intermediate stations, funiculars with mid-stops), the stack is centered on the stop's projected position along the transit line. Neither case needs a configured "extent" length: aerial has no natural extent and doesn't need one — the anchor rules above (endpoint at terminals, projected stop at non-terminals) plus the pill-arrow lengths themselves fully determine placement. Funicular's existing extent from stop/dot placement is unused for these anchors; it remains untouched only so the debug overlay and other consumers keep working.
- **Ferry**: pier is point-like, no platform extent from stop/dot placement. The pill-arrow's back-cap sits **10 m in the direction of travel from the ferry pill's on-line position** (see `pill-rendering.md § Ferry stops` for that base position), with the chevron pointing further forward. If a pier serves multiple lines, they queue backward from that anchor toward the pier.
- **Offset measurement**: for bus, tram, and other side-anchored modes, pills sit beside the line, shifted sideways by the fixed clear gap plus half the pill width; stepping, spans and gaps are measured along that sideways-shifted course, not along the centerline — measured on the centerline, every degree of bend stretches the gaps on the outside of a curve and squeezes them on the inside. Rail-style modes (train + all mountain + ferry + hybrid tram — see below) have no sideways offset: pills sit **on** the stop position line itself, so stepping is measured along the line directly.
- **Anchor**: derived from the **stop position line alone** — the raw GTFS stop coordinate is never consulted for placement (it only feeds the stop-line computation itself and the direction tangents). Road-mode queues anchor the lead pill's chevron tip at the line's **forward end** (the vehicle pulled fully forward — canonical case: the PostAuto station sawtooth bays in Bern); rail stacks center on the line's **middle**, except at end-of-platform rail terminals (see `stop-extent-osm-walk.md` § Rail walk case 2), where the stack anchors the fastest pill-arrow at the extent's **buffer end** and queues inward — the pill-arrows sit against the physical end of the tracks, matching the way trains actually stand at a stub terminus. Non-rail keeps the extent's own point order because its front end IS the stop end by construction. For rail the stop position line is normalised to align with the fastest cluster's direction of travel; a borrowed slice running the other way — arrival counterpart at a terminus, longer opposite-direction sibling picked by the same-curb union — would otherwise mirror every chevron on the platform. (An earlier travel-direction-based reversal was applied to non-rail too and removed: it misfired wherever the vehicle turns right after the stop, and a vehicle may legitimately stand rotated up to 180° from its onward travel direction at a turnaround, so onward direction can never orient the platform.) Pill-arrows are always parallel to the debug stop line and sit on it — even when the underlying geometry is wrong (broken routing, e.g. Agno), they are wrong together, consistently.
- **Zero-extent queue orientation**: when a stop's own debug line has length 0 (the departure's extent collapsed — no ground exists behind the departing vehicle), the queue sits on borrowed ground (typically the arrival counterpart's stop position line), whose point order does not carry the departure's direction. For exactly these queues, each pill-arrow's direction is checked against **the exact segment of line it runs along**: project the pill-arrow's own span onto the departing line's polyline and take the departure's direction of travel along that segment; when the course orientation opposes it, the pill-arrow flips **in place** (chevron and text turn 180°, position unchanged). When the departing line does not run along that segment, no direction can be derived — the pill-arrow is inherently ambiguous there and keeps the course orientation. Stops whose own debug line is non-empty are untouched by this rule. Because the reference is the segment under the pill-arrow itself — never a tangent at the stop — turnaround-skewed departure tangents cannot misfire; this is deliberately NOT the removed travel-direction reversal. Canonical case: Les Crosets, télésièges — the 81 departure has no rear ground (dead-end road at the chairlift), the queue sits on the arrival's extent pointing up the dead end, and the flip turns it toward where the departing bus actually drives.
- **Side of the line**: bus and tram always to the right in direction of travel; **rail-style modes (train + all mountain + ferry + hybrid tram — see below) sit centered on the line itself, no sideways offset** — there is no platform side. (An earlier rule placed rail pill-arrows on the side the GTFS stop position snapped from; retired because the snap side is wrong for ~10–30% of stops, and narrow platforms with tracks on both sides produced overlapping stacks bleeding in from the neighbouring track. Centering loses the platform-side signal but eliminates both failure modes at once — the map no longer attempts to show which side of the track passengers stand on, because the underlying data is not good enough to do so honestly.)
- **Direction grouping**: pills at a stop heading the same way (within 45°) form one stack. Direction is derived from the line tangent at the stop, not from GTFS direction fields.
- **Same-curb / same-track resolution**: same-direction groups of the same parent station (UIC) can end up on the same ground under different GTFS platform ids. Non-rail canonical case is Bern, Schanzenstrasse, where southbound city bus 20 stops at platform `:10001` and southbound regional 100/101 at `:10000`, a few metres apart on one curb. Rail canonical case is Bern's mainline tracks, whose per-track queues get drawn on top of each other. Whenever two such queues' stop position lines run laterally closer than a mode-specific tolerance — **2 m for tram / bus / regional_bus**, **1 m for rail** — they are resolved by their along-line overlap, measured against the shorter line:
  - **Overlap > 30%** → merge: one stop position line covering the **union** of both ranges, and one stop — the visits pool and the standard collapse / stacking logic runs on the merged set. The union extent covers the longest possible span across the pooled tracks. For rail, the merged queue's stack is centered on the middle of the union extent. Merging is transitive (chains all merge) and runs before the shortening pass.
  - **Overlap ≤ 30%** → both stop position lines are shortened **symmetrically to the middle of the overlapping section**, so they just touch. For non-rail (tip-at-front anchor): if a queue no longer fits in its shortened range, it extends **the other way** — the stack may overflow forward past the line's front end (pills in front of the stop are better than pills overlapping the neighbour); this is the one deliberate exception to the tip-at-the-front anchor rule. For rail (centered anchor): the shortened range still centers the stack on its middle, so any overflow is symmetric around the new middle.
  - Long merged queues are acceptable.
- **Shared path for parallel lines**: when lines in one direction group run on parallel but non-overlapping map geometries (e.g. tram + bus on the same street), every pill-arrow in the group follows the group's **rightmost** line, so they line up on one path; the group's stop position line is taken from that line. (A previous rule preferring lines whose geometry covers the stack's span is obsolete — the stop position line is always fitted within real geometry, so terminal lines no longer produce guesswork axes.)
- **Rail platform-sector merge**: rail stop_ids at one parent station whose `platform_code` shares the same numeric leading-digit run (e.g. `7`, `7A-C`, `7D-F` all reduce to `7`) refer to one physical track and are pooled into one close-zoom queue for that track. The pooled queue uses the **full platform extent** for the prefix; sector-specific sub-extents are ignored so trains that only cover part of the platform still queue on the same axis as trains covering the full platform. This means the per-track pool that the rail rules below operate on is a numeric-platform-prefix group of stop_ids, not necessarily a single stop_id.
- **Rail-style (train + all mountain + ferry + hybrid tram)**: all pill-arrows at one stop (after the sector merge and same-curb merge above, where applicable) form **one** stack sitting **on** the stop position line itself (no sideways offset), centered along the middle of that line — overriding the generic direction-grouping rule (opposite directions do **not** form a separate stack). This applies to trains and rack rail (numeric platform codes drive the sector merge), aerial and funicular mountain modes (single station serves both directions of the cable), ferries (a pier is a point-like docking structure with no side concept — the boat pulls up to it), and tram stops flagged as hybrid. Pills within the stack are ordered by direction of travel so **no two adjacent pills point at each other**: same-direction pills stay contiguous, and each direction sub-group's chevron tips point outward toward that direction's end of the platform. Within each direction sub-group, fastest line sits furthest forward (toward that sub-group's outward end). The stack's overall center coincides with the platform middle regardless of how the counts split between directions — 7 pills one way + 1 pill the other is centered on the platform middle, not on the boundary between the sub-groups.
- **Hybrid tram detection**: tram-classified lines (GTFS `route_type` 900) that run on train-like track infrastructure behave as **rail** at exactly the stops where they do so. The check is **per-stop**: if the tram's shaped geometry at the stop lies within a tight tolerance (1–2 m) of an OSM way tagged `railway=narrow_gauge` or `railway=light_rail`, that stop instance uses the full rail rule set (on-line placement, per-track pooling via the sector merge, no direction split, one centered stack, rail's 1 m same-curb lateral tolerance). At transitions where both `tram` and `narrow_gauge` / `light_rail` ways are nearby, the way closest to the stop's projected position on the shape wins. A tram stop whose geometry lies on a `railway=tram` way keeps the tram rules (right-side offset, direction-split, curbside stack). Per-stop is required because hybrid lines can and do mix within one route: the canonical case, Forchbahn, runs on Zürich's shared tram network in its inner-city section and on its own narrow-gauge alignment in the outer section, so the same operator's stops split between behaviors along the route. The check is applied universally to every tram stop (no operator whitelist), so any newly-hybrid line is picked up automatically without a config change.
- **All other modes**: the fastest line's chevron tip lands at the forward end of the stop position line; slower lines queue upstream behind it.
- **Stack gap**: a fixed polygon-edge-to-polygon-edge gap between consecutive pill-arrows, tuned so the visible outside-border-to-outside-border distance (after the centered 0.4 m border eats 0.2 m from each side) stays clearly readable at close zoom without pills touching.
- **Sorting metric for "fastest"**: frequency (`f_weighted`) for tram / bus / regional_bus — a rare short-turn variant must not out-rank the through variants and hijack the merged pill's geometry — and the per-line speed value for rail-like modes. Applies to both the variant-representative pick and the stacking order.

### Station background polygon

One translucent polygon per parent station, sitting behind the transit lines (not just behind the stop layers).

- **Shape**: a rounded convex envelope ("hull") around everything the station comprises: all pill-arrow outlines, the line sections adjacent to them, and — for rail — the **full platform extent** along the track (the same platform-extent logic used by the platform debug overlay), plus a fixed outward padding. The outline only bulges outward; it never notches inward between platforms. Covering somewhat more than the exact union is fine.
- No overlapping shapes — exactly one polygon per parent station.
- **Color**: the serving line's color. When lines of several colors serve the station, a blend of all their colors stands in for a gradient (a true polygon gradient fill is not possible in the rendering engine).

## Retired approaches (do not retry without new renderer capabilities)

Three alternative rendering architectures were fully implemented and reverted (July 2026):

- **Curved bodies** — polygon following the line geometry. Labels cannot curve with the shape, so text and body angles diverge on bent lines; replaced by straight bodies on one shared axis per stack.
- **Sprite/symbol pills** — pixel-fixed pills as SDF icons with slot-quantized offsets. Blockers: vector tiles cannot carry per-feature arrays (2D offsets must be quantized into match-expression slot tables); MapLibre multiplies icon-offset by icon-size and text-offset by text-size, silently rescaling tuned gaps whenever size curves change; pixel-fixed pills slide across the map while zooming (deep stacks move wildly relative to their stop).
- **Line-rendered bodies** — body as a stroked LineString (metre length × pixel height via line-offset), disc/tip/labels as symbols. The rotation-frame behavior of symbol offsets vs. icons proved unreliable in practice: misaligned discs and text, detached tips.

Renderer facts learned: `line-offset` and rotation values are per-feature numerics and safe to data-drive; array-valued layout properties are not; a real polygon gradient fill does not exist; per-character advance widths for build-time text measuring live in `glyph_widths.json`.

## Constraints

- Mountain (all sub-types: aerial, funicular, rack, rebucketed_rail) and ferry both use the rail-style placement rules — on-line, no sideways offset, single centered stack per stop, no direction split. (Previously aerial and funicular followed the bus/tram-style right-side rules, and ferry defaulted to the road-mode anchor; both retired for the same data-quality / no-real-side-concept reason as train.)
- Band A→B→C sizes, fonts, margins, gaps, and the backdrop padding are seed-and-refine — tune after visual review, but keep the band tables in the pipeline and in the style generator in sync.
- The build-time text wrapping replaces renderer-side wrapping entirely — the renderer must not re-wrap.
- Do not reintroduce an `intercity` bucket or any other mode key when extending the renderer.
